The UAE Property Market: Six-Month Review

Reporting period: 8 May – 8 November 2026 · Special edition published November 2026

Over the past six months, Abu Dhabi’s property market has continued to attract substantial investment, while the latest figures point to a more selective market. Strong year-on-year price growth has been accompanied by early signs of quarterly moderation, a temporary rent-increase freeze, new compliance requirements and a growing wave of residential handovers.

1. Market activity: a strong first half, followed by a more measured third quarter

According to the Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre (ADREC), total real estate transactions reached AED 117 billion in the first half of 2026, up 112% year-on-year, with transaction volumes increasing 61.7%. Sales accounted for AED 86.1 billion across 16,838 transactions, while mortgage transactions totalled AED 26.7 billion. Foreign direct investment reached AED 13.8 billion, an increase of 309% year-on-year, with investors from 116 nationalities active in the market.

Eight additional investment zones were approved during the first half, bringing Abu Dhabi’s total to 50. ADREC also registered 28 new real estate projects. These figures underline the depth of investor interest, although headline transaction value alone does not mean every location or property type is performing equally.

2. Sales prices and buyer trends

By the third quarter, the market was showing a more mixed picture. Cushman & Wakefield Core reported a 1% quarter-on-quarter decline in city-wide residential sale prices, while values remained 22% higher year-on-year. Savills recorded 6,085 residential transactions in Q3, 19% lower quarter-on-quarter, with off-plan homes accounting for 81% of activity. Other research providers reported different price measures and growth rates, reflecting differences in their datasets and methodologies.

Apartment performance remained comparatively strong in several established communities. Q3 research highlighted annual apartment price increases in areas including Saadiyat Island, Al Reem Island, Al Reef Downtown and Al Raha Beach. Villa performance was more varied: some communities recorded solid annual gains while others experienced price corrections. This makes building quality, service charges, supply pipeline, location and achievable rent increasingly important when comparing opportunities.

Off-plan continued to play a major role. ADREC’s H1 residential report put residential unit sales at AED 70.4 billion, with off-plan accounting for 89% of sales value. In Q3, Savills reported that off-plan represented 81% of residential transaction activity. Buyers should still assess developer track record, escrow arrangements, payment milestones, delivery dates and the likely supply of competing units before committing.

3. Rental market: the temporary 0% increase measure

On 3 June 2026, ADREC announced a temporary change to Abu Dhabi’s annual rental increase percentage, reducing it from 5% to 0% per annum until further notice. The measure applies to residential, commercial and industrial tenancy renewals. ADREC stated that renewals and new agreements should reference the rental rate in the property’s last registered Tawtheeq contract.

The measure was introduced after a period of strong rental growth. ADREC’s H1 report recorded 233,000 active residential lease contracts and AED 9.3 billion in lease values. New-lease prices had risen year-on-year, particularly in investment zones. By Q3, Cushman & Wakefield Core reported city-wide residential rents down 5% quarter-on-quarter but still 4% higher year-on-year. Other providers reported more stable rental measures. The differences reinforce the need to compare like-for-like properties and distinguish new-lease asking rents from registered renewals.

Practical note: landlords, tenants and investors should check the latest ADREC guidance and their registered Tawtheeq records before relying on a rental figure or assuming that a temporary measure has ended. This newsletter is general information, not legal advice.

4. Regulatory and systems update

Abu Dhabi occupancy certificates: On 5 August 2026, the Department of Municipalities and Transport announced a grace period until 16 September 2026 for property owners and operators to apply for or renew occupancy certificates through the Municipal e-Permitting System. The programme includes checks such as structural stability, Civil Defence and fire safety, gas systems, water and plumbing, and HVAC and ventilation. ADREC stated that new Tawtheeq lease registrations would be blocked for units in buildings without a valid occupancy certificate. Owners and property managers should confirm the current compliance status of their buildings.

Off-plan mortgages: ADREC’s off-plan mortgage registration framework became operational during 2026. Under the framework reported in September, eligible purchasers who have paid 50% of a unit’s price may be able to arrange mortgage financing during construction, subject to the participating developer, lender and applicable requirements. Mortgage interests can be recorded in the Initial Real Estate Register. This may give qualifying buyers more flexibility before handover, but finance approval and eligibility must be confirmed directly with the bank and developer.

Dubai digital registration: On 3 September 2026, Dubai Land Department launched its Initial Registration platform to bring project registration, real estate transaction registration and escrow account management into a more connected digital process. Its Project 360 function is intended to provide developers with a consolidated view of project units, escrow accounts, financial information and records. This is a Dubai system initiative and should not be confused with Abu Dhabi’s ADREC or Tawtheeq processes.

5. Investment returns and ROI: what has changed?

Rental yield remains highly dependent on purchase price, achievable rent, unit condition, service charges, vacancy, management costs and financing. Q3 2026 asking-price and asking-rent analysis published by Bayut indicated gross apartment yield examples of approximately 7.9% in Al Reef, 7.0% on Al Maryah Island, 7.1% in Al Samha and 6.7% in Masdar City. Its reported villa yield examples varied widely by segment and location, from around 4.3% in the ultra-luxury Saadiyat segment to higher quoted yields in selected mid-market areas.

These are indicative gross yields based on listing data, not guaranteed returns and not net ROI. They do not automatically account for service charges, maintenance, vacancy, leasing fees, furnishing, financing costs or purchase and sale expenses. A property with a lower headline yield may still suit a long-term capital-growth strategy, while a higher gross yield may come with greater maintenance, vacancy or resale risk.

With the temporary rental-increase measure in place and more supply expected, investors should stress-test rent assumptions rather than simply extrapolating the rapid rental growth of previous years. Compare current achievable rent with the total acquisition cost and calculate net operating income before deciding.

6. New homes handed over and the supply pipeline

Cushman & Wakefield Core reported that 2,545 residential units had been delivered in Abu Dhabi during the first nine months of 2026. Q3 completions were led in part by Aldar projects including The Grove – Heart on Saadiyat Island and Yas Park Views – Noya 5. A further 4,412 units were expected in Q4, although the consultancy cautioned that some scheduled completions could move into 2027.

The same research highlighted a substantial pipeline, with more than 52,000 units scheduled for delivery between 2027 and 2029. ADREC’s H1 report separately projected approximately 71,000 additional residential units across the emirate by 2030, with annual deliveries expected to peak in 2028. These figures use different coverage and forecasting assumptions, so they should not be added together. For buyers and investors, the key question is how much new stock will arrive in the specific community and property segment they are considering.

7. What this means for buyers, sellers, landlords and investors

  • Buyers: compare completed and off-plan options carefully, check building and occupancy documentation, and review the full cost of ownership.
  • Investors: use realistic rent assumptions, calculate net rather than headline gross yield, and assess future competing supply.
  • Landlords: keep Tawtheeq records and building compliance documents up to date, and confirm current rental rules before issuing renewal terms.
  • Sellers: price against recent comparable transactions and the condition of competing available stock, not only historic peak asking prices.
  • Off-plan purchasers: verify project registration, escrow protections, contractual milestones and any lender eligibility before relying on construction-stage finance.

Our outlook

Abu Dhabi remains an active and internationally recognised property market, supported by investment, population and business activity. However, the second half of 2026 has highlighted a transition from broad-based rapid growth towards a market where location, pricing, building quality and delivery timing matter more. With additional homes expected to enter the market, careful research and realistic financial modelling will be essential.

At Royal Bridges, we encourage buyers, owners and investors to look beyond headlines and assess each property on its own merits. Market conditions can change, and past performance is not a guarantee of future returns.
