ARTICLE 01

Why Invest in the UAE?

The UAE has become an important destination for international investors, entrepreneurs and people looking for a new place to call home. Its property market offers a variety of opportunities, from city apartments and family villas to waterfront homes and off-plan developments.

What attracts property investors?

  • A range of residential and commercial property options.
  • Established business and international transport connections.
  • Different property ownership arrangements, depending on location and eligibility.
  • Communities offering different lifestyles, facilities and price points.
  • Opportunities to consider both completed and off-plan property.

What should you consider before investing?

Understand the location, purchase costs, service charges, expected rental demand, maintenance costs and resale considerations. If you are considering an off-plan purchase, review the developer, project documentation, payment plan and completion provisions carefully.

Property investment carries risks. Rental income, capital appreciation and future resale values are not guaranteed. Independent legal and financial advice can help you assess whether a particular purchase is appropriate for your circumstances.

Royal Bridges tip: Start with your investment objective. A property suited to long-term rental income may be very different from one chosen for personal use or future resale.

ARTICLE 02

Abu Dhabi or Dubai: Which Is Right for You?

Both Abu Dhabi and Dubai offer attractive property and lifestyle options, but they are not identical. The right choice depends on your employment, family requirements, budget, preferred lifestyle and investment goals.

Why consider Abu Dhabi?

Abu Dhabi offers a range of established and emerging residential communities, including Yas Island, Saadiyat Island, Al Reem Island and Al Raha Beach. It may appeal to people who want access to cultural attractions, family communities, business districts and coastal living.

Why consider Dubai?

Dubai has a large and varied property market, with established areas such as Downtown Dubai, Dubai Marina, Palm Jumeirah and Dubai Hills Estate. It offers a wide range of residential, commercial and lifestyle choices.

Compare more than the property price

  • Your daily commute and workplace location.
  • School availability and travel time for children.
  • Service charges and ongoing property costs.
  • Access to transport, healthcare and amenities.
  • Your preferred community atmosphere and lifestyle.

There is no universal winner. Visit the areas, compare actual properties and consider your day-to-day needs before deciding.

Royal Bridges tip: A beautiful property is only part of the decision. Think about the community and the life you want to build around it.

ARTICLE 03

What Happens After You Choose a Property With Your Agent?

Finding the right property is an exciting first step. Once you decide to proceed, there are several practical stages before you receive the keys.

For a rental property

  • Confirm the agreed rent, payment schedule, deposit and any agency fees.
  • Review the tenancy terms and confirm the names of all relevant parties.
  • Provide the required identification and supporting documents.
  • Arrange payment and the tenancy contract in accordance with the applicable process.
  • Complete any required tenancy registration, such as Tawtheeq in Abu Dhabi.
  • Arrange utilities and any required access or move-in permissions.
  • Inspect the property, document its condition and collect the keys.

For a purchase

  • Confirm the property details, ownership and agreed purchase price.
  • Review the memorandum of understanding or relevant sale documentation.
  • Confirm the deposit, payment arrangements and transaction conditions.
  • Complete required checks, mortgage arrangements and approvals where applicable.
  • Arrange transfer through the relevant authority or registration process.
  • Confirm handover, keys and any outstanding property matters.

The exact process varies according to the property, transaction type, financing arrangements and applicable authority requirements. Ask your agent to explain each stage and provide a clear breakdown of expected costs.

Royal Bridges tip: Do not feel rushed into signing. Read the documents, understand the payment obligations and ask questions before committing.

ARTICLE 04

How Do I Know My Agent Is Legitimate and My Transaction Is Safe?

Buying or renting property involves significant financial commitments. Taking time to verify the people and documents involved can help reduce the risk of fraud and misunderstandings.

What should you check?

  • Ask for the agent's professional identification and relevant registration details.
  • Verify the brokerage or agency through the relevant regulatory authority.
  • Confirm who owns the property and whether the person instructing the sale or lease is authorised.
  • Check the property details and relevant ownership or project documentation.
  • Make sure agreements clearly identify the parties, price, payment terms and responsibilities.
  • Use verified payment instructions and confirm the beneficiary before transferring funds.
  • Keep copies of signed documents, receipts and written communications.

Be careful with payments

Do not rely solely on a WhatsApp message or an emailed change of bank details. Independently verify payment instructions using a trusted contact method. Be cautious if someone pressures you to transfer money urgently or to an unrelated personal account.

If something does not look right, pause the transaction and seek independent advice before proceeding.

Royal Bridges tip: A professional transaction should be transparent. You should understand who is receiving your money, why it is being paid and what document supports the payment.
ARTICLE 05

I Know the Buyer and Want to Sell Without an Agent. Can I Get Help With the MOU and Mortgage Procedures?

It is possible for a buyer and seller to identify each other directly. However, agreeing a price is only one part of completing a property transaction. The documentation, financing and transfer arrangements still need careful attention.

What support might be needed?

  • Preparing or reviewing the relevant sale documentation.
  • Confirming the buyer and seller details and property information.
  • Recording the agreed price, deposit and completion conditions.
  • Coordinating with the buyer's mortgage bank where financing is involved.
  • Checking whether the seller has an existing mortgage or other obligations affecting transfer.
  • Understanding the applicable fees, clearances and transfer requirements.

Why mortgage transactions need extra coordination

A mortgage-funded purchase may involve a bank valuation, finance approval, property checks and coordination between the buyer's bank, seller and relevant authorities. The timing and requirements depend on the bank and the specific transaction.

Both parties should understand their contractual obligations and obtain independent legal advice where appropriate. Do not assume a private agreement alone completes the legal transfer of ownership.

Royal Bridges tip: Even when the buyer and seller already know each other, having the transaction properly documented and coordinated can help prevent avoidable delays.

ARTICLE 06

Which Is the Best Bank to Secure a Mortgage With in the UAE?

There is no single mortgage bank that is best for everyone. Different lenders offer different products, eligibility criteria, fees and rates. The most suitable option depends on your income, employment, residency status, deposit and property choice.

What should you compare?

  • Interest or profit rate and whether it is fixed, variable or introductory.
  • Arrangement, valuation and other bank fees.
  • Early settlement and partial repayment conditions.
  • Required salary transfer and minimum income.
  • Loan-to-value limits and deposit requirements.
  • Mortgage term, monthly payment and total borrowing cost.
  • How the bank handles off-plan or completed property purchases.

Ask for a personalised illustration

Ask more than one lender or a qualified mortgage adviser to provide a written illustration based on your actual circumstances. A headline rate may not reflect the total cost of borrowing.

Royal Bridges tip: Compare the full mortgage offer, not just the monthly payment or advertised rate. Bank products and eligibility criteria can change.
ARTICLE 07

Am I Eligible for a Mortgage in the UAE?

Mortgage eligibility is assessed individually by each lender. Your income, employment, residency, existing debts and the property itself can all affect the outcome.

What will a bank usually consider?

  • Your monthly income and how it is evidenced.
  • Employment status, employer details or business income.
  • Existing loans, credit cards and other financial commitments.
  • Your credit history and repayment record.
  • Your available deposit and source of funds.
  • Your residency or non-resident status, where relevant.
  • The property type, value and location.

Documents you may be asked to provide

Depending on the lender and your circumstances, these may include identification, visa and Emirates ID details, salary certificates, bank statements, employment documents or business financial records.

Do not commit to a purchase on the assumption that finance will be approved. Obtain an assessment from the lender and understand the conditions attached to any approval.

Royal Bridges tip: Speak to a mortgage specialist early in your property search so you can understand your likely budget and the documents you may need.
ARTICLE 08

Why Is a Move-In and Move-Out Inspection So Important?

A property inspection at the beginning and end of a tenancy creates a record of the property's condition. It can help landlords and tenants understand what was already present and what may have changed during the tenancy.

What should a move-in inspection cover?

  • Walls, ceilings, floors and paintwork.
  • Doors, windows, locks and keys.
  • Kitchen cupboards, appliances and fittings.
  • Bathrooms, taps, toilets and visible plumbing.
  • Air conditioning and visible electrical fittings.
  • Furniture and inventory items, where applicable.
  • Existing damage, marks, defects or maintenance concerns.

Why document everything?

Clear photographs, videos and a written condition report can help avoid disagreements later. Both parties should keep copies and raise any concerns promptly through the agreed communication process.

At move-out, compare the condition with the original record, taking account of the tenancy agreement, applicable law and fair wear and tear. Inspection procedures and obligations can vary, so check the terms that apply to your tenancy.

Royal Bridges tip: Take dated photographs before moving furniture and belongings into the property. Keep your inspection report somewhere you can find it when you move out.

ARTICLE 09

Ship My Furniture or Buy New After Relocating to the UAE?

One of the biggest decisions when relocating is what to do with your existing furniture and household belongings. Should you ship everything, bring only the essentials or start again once you arrive?

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. The best decision depends on the quality of your furniture, shipping costs, your destination home, the length of your stay and how much you value keeping familiar belongings.

Option 1: Ship your existing furniture

Shipping may make sense when you have high-quality furniture, sentimental items or belongings that would be expensive to replace.

Consider:

  • International shipping and packing costs.
  • Insurance and customs requirements.
  • Collection and delivery arrangements.
  • Storage costs if your new home is not ready.
  • Whether your furniture will suit the size and layout of your new property.
  • How long the shipment may take to arrive.

Option 2: Buy new furniture in the UAE

Buying locally can be convenient if you want to choose furniture that fits your new home. It may also avoid the complexity of an international shipment.

Consider:

  • The cost of furnishing an entire property.
  • Delivery and installation charges.
  • Availability and delivery times.
  • Whether you are buying for a permanent home or a temporary stay.
  • What you will do with the furniture if you relocate again.

Option 3: Take a hybrid approach

For many families, the best solution is a combination. Ship selected personal or valuable items, bring essential belongings and purchase larger furniture locally.

Do a proper cost comparison

Ask international movers for itemised quotations and compare the total landed cost with the cost of buying equivalent furniture in the UAE. Include delivery, installation, insurance, storage and any applicable customs charges.

Royal Bridges tip: Before arranging a shipment, secure your new property and check its measurements. Your favourite sofa may not fit the lift, doorway or living room in your new home!
