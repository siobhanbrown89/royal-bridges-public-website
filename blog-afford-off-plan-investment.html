Can I Afford an Off-Plan Investment?

The most suitable off-plan property is not necessarily the one in the most fashionable location. A payment plan may fit your cash flow better, but affordability means being able to meet every instalment and still manage your wider financial commitments.

Off-Plan & Finance

Compare the payment schedule with your actual cash flow

Map every instalment against its due date, not just the initial booking amount. Include the reservation deposit, SPA payment, construction-linked instalments, any post-handover payments and the final balance. Confirm whether instalments are linked to construction milestones or fixed calendar dates.

Budget for costs beyond the advertised price

Allow for registration and administrative charges, agency fees where applicable, mortgage costs if borrowing, furnishing, service charges, utilities and moving costs. Ask the developer for a written schedule of all charges and verify the applicable fees independently.

Test affordability under pressure

Keep an emergency reserve. Model a delay in income, a change in interest rates if you plan to borrow, currency movements if your income is not in AED, and a period when you cannot rent or resell the unit. Do not rely on future appreciation or an assumed resale to fund instalments.

Location versus payment-plan flexibility

A flexible plan can make a purchase easier to schedule, but it does not automatically make the property better value. Compare location, developer track record, build quality, service-charge estimates, expected supply, rental demand, resale restrictions and total cost. A convenient plan should not outweigh weak fundamentals.

A simple affordability test

After paying each instalment, can you still meet normal living costs, existing debt repayments and emergency savings? If not, the commitment may be too stretched. Ask an independent mortgage adviser or qualified financial professional to review your funding plan before signing.
