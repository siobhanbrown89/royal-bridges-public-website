Selling Without an Agent: Help With the MOU and Mortgage Procedures

A private sale can be possible, but the seller and buyer still need to manage documentation, finance, clearances and registration correctly. Procedures vary by emirate and by whether the property has an existing mortgage.

Selling Property

Agree the commercial terms first

Record the property details, agreed price, deposit, completion date, included items and any conditions. Use the correct official forms or a professionally reviewed agreement for the relevant emirate.

Prepare the property documents

Gather ownership details, identification, service-charge statements, tenancy information if occupied, and any required developer or community documents. Confirm whether there are outstanding amounts or restrictions.

If the seller has a mortgage

Ask the lending bank for its liability letter and settlement process. The bank may require a buyer's bank or cash settlement arrangement, and the mortgage release and transfer must be coordinated. Obtain written instructions from the bank before agreeing a completion date.

If the buyer needs a mortgage

The buyer will usually need lender approval and a valuation. The bank's conditions and timing can affect the transaction. Do not assume a pre-approval guarantees funds will be released.

NOC, clearances and transfer

Depending on the emirate and property, a developer or community NOC, service-charge clearance, mortgage release and land registration appointment may be required. Confirm the current checklist with the relevant authority.

Get independent document support

A private sale does not remove the need for accurate paperwork. Consider a qualified conveyancing professional or UAE lawyer to review the MOU, payment flow and mortgage arrangements.

Important: This article is general educational information, not legal, mortgage, tax or investment advice. Laws, fees and procedures can change and may differ between emirates, projects and individual contracts. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and obtain independent professional advice before making a decision.
